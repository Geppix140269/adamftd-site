02 · The MVP, Plainly Stated
Chapter Two · MVP

The MVP, Plainly Stated

The platform described in this book is a Minimum Viable Product. It went live at the end of March 2026. It is in production. It is what over a thousand users in 30+ countries are using today.
What 'MVP' means here

A working product, not a prototype

All thirteen tools and 97 capabilities across 24 categories are operational. The AI is in production. Real users are running real trade queries against real data. The product works end‑to‑end.

What is deliberately out of scope

Polish, not capability

The MVP scope was: prove the grounded AI thesis at scale. UX refinement, onboarding flow, mobile responsiveness, and Market Reports templates are scheduled · they are not the question the MVP needed to answer.

Why we shipped it now

The window does not wait

Geopolitics is making trade intelligence existential. The market expects AI for complex queries. Shipping the MVP at end of March 2026 captured the moment. Two months in, the validation answer is clear: people find it, log in, and use it.

02.1What 'MVP' is not

The word minimum viable product is, in 2026, often a euphemism for "early demo." That is not what is described in this book. The ADAMftd MVP is a production system, deployed and operated by a senior engineering bench, hosted on funded infrastructure (NVIDIA $1M compute credit award), and used daily by people who arrived without being told to.

What makes it minimum is the deliberate narrowness of the polish layer. The data pipeline is complete. The AI is grounded. The five intelligence layers are wired through. Every tool answers the question it was built to answer, cites its source, and refuses to invent. What it does not yet do is hold the hand of a brand‑new user through their first ten minutes · that is the next sprint, post‑seed.

What makes it viable is that, in two months and with no marketing budget, over a thousand users from 30+ countries chose to register and use it. Sixty‑four of them consumed every starter credit available. That is what viability looks like, in product terms.

MVP, not demo. A real product, in production, used by real people, with no marketing. Two months in.

05 · Grounded AI at Scale
Chapter Five · Architecture

Under the Hood: Grounded AI at Scale

Every answer follows a strict five‑step verification pipeline. The AI does not respond until it has cross‑referenced, conflict‑resolved, and cited its sources. This is what 'grounded' means in practice.
Fig. 05.1 · The five‑step pipeline
Step 01
User Query
Natural‑language question entered. Any language. Any trade topic. The AI parses intent and identifies the data domains required.
Step 02
Four‑Source Pull
Data pulled from four independent official sources simultaneously · UN Comtrade, customs registries, official tariff databases, sanctions lists, port authorities.
Step 03
Conflict Detection
Sources compared. Outliers identified. When sources disagree, the conflict is flagged · not silently resolved. Transparency first.
Step 04
Monte Carlo Resolution
Our proprietary models plus Monte Carlo simulation calculate the most likely correct answer when sources conflict. The math is shown.
Step 05
Cited Answer
The answer is returned with every source cited. The user sees not just the answer but where it came from and how confident the system is.
The Methodology Disclaimer · Canonical

"ADAMftd verifies against multiple official sources, but occasionally these sources may conflict with one another. In such instances we identify outlying data first, and then run Monte Carlo models alongside our own proprietary models to calculate the most likely outcome. AI can make mistakes."

Shown to the user · every session

05.1What this means for users

  • You never get a hallucinated answer without knowing it is uncertain.
  • Every claim has a source you can verify independently.
  • Conflicts are disclosed · the AI earns trust by being honest about uncertainty.
  • Multiple frontier models run in parallel with an independent math cross‑check.
  • The audit trail is complete and exportable.

06 · Hormuz Simulator
Chapter Six · Flagship

The Hormuz Simulator

The world's only HS‑6 oil shock scenario engine. Models the impact of a Strait of Hormuz disruption · full, partial, or negotiated · across thousands of commodity codes and every affected trade corridor.
Why this exists

The Strait of Hormuz carries twenty million barrels of oil per day · roughly twenty‑five percent of global seaborne oil. A disruption is not theoretical risk. It is recurring policy reality. Commodity traders, banks, insurance underwriters, and government agencies need a way to model exposure quantitatively, by commodity, by corridor, by counterparty.

Before ADAMftd, this required bespoke consulting engagements. The Hormuz Simulator industrialises it.

07 · Market Analysis Suite
Chapter Seven · Flagship

Market Analysis · Eleven Functions, Two Bands

The only natural-language trade-readiness assessment suite at this depth and this price. Two analysis bands, Market Overview and Trade Readiness, at two credits per function. The same work runs five figures and weeks of analyst time anywhere else.
Band One · Market Overview

Understand the destination market before you ship anything.

01 Retail Snapshot

Where the product sells, who sells it, and at what price.

  • Channel mix: modern trade vs. traditional, online vs. offline
  • Leading retailers in the destination market
  • Private-label exposure and shelf competition
  • Headline price bands for the category
02 Market Size and Demand

Addressable volume and value for the product in the destination market.

  • Total addressable market in volume and value
  • Five-year demand trajectory and growth pockets
  • Demand segments and their relative size
  • Import share vs. domestic supply
03 Consumer Preferences

What the local buyer actually wants and why.

  • Preferred flavour, format, and pack size
  • Brand affinity and trust signals
  • Willingness-to-pay drivers
  • Emerging preference shifts in the category
04 Sentiment Analysis

The current pulse of buyer attitude in your category.

  • Sentiment scoring across review platforms
  • Social and forum signal in the destination
  • Recent reputational shifts for the category
  • Negative triggers and trust breakers
05 Seasonal Demand Intelligence

When demand peaks, troughs, and shifts.

  • Quarter-by-quarter demand intensity map
  • Holiday and religious-calendar effects
  • Climate-driven seasonality patterns
  • Promotional windows and off-season risk
06 Local Production Overview

Domestic supply, capacity, and competitive pricing.

  • Who already produces this in the destination
  • Domestic scale and capacity utilisation
  • Landed cost of local product vs. your import
  • Subsidy or protection exposure
Band Two · Trade Readiness

Determine whether you are ready to enter, and how to win.

07 Substitutes and Competitors

What you are fighting against on shelf and online.

  • Direct rivals and adjacent substitutes
  • Price ladder and positioning gaps
  • Distribution footprint of key competitors
  • Import brands already in the market
08 SWOT Analysis

Structured strategic positioning vs. the destination market.

  • Strengths and weaknesses against incumbents
  • Opportunities specific to the entry path
  • Threats from local and regional competitors
  • Actionable strategic recommendation
09 Market Entry Barriers

Regulatory, tariff, distribution, and cultural.

  • Tariff rates, quotas, and preferential routes
  • Regulatory approval timelines
  • Distribution lock-in and exclusivity norms
  • Cultural and consumer-trust barriers
10 Packaging and Labelling

Compliance and presentation requirements.

  • Mandatory marks and language requirements
  • Unit and pack-size norms for the channel
  • Labelling claims that are prohibited locally
  • Origin and traceability display rules
11 Quality Standards and Certifications

What your product must carry to clear customs and sell.

  • Mandatory certifications for market entry
  • Recognised testing and accreditation bodies
  • Equivalence routes from origin-country certs
  • Time-to-certification and typical cost
