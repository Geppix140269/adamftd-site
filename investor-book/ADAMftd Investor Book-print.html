02The ask

We are raising $2.5M to $4M on a SAFE at a $20M post‑money cap, with a $20M pre‑money anchor. Minimum check $250,000; lead investor preferred at $500k to $1M with pro‑rata rights.

SAFE · Simple Agreement for Future Equity. A standard early‑stage instrument from Y Combinator: investors wire cash now and convert into shares automatically at the next priced round (Series A), at the agreed valuation cap. No interest, no maturity, no negotiation of share terms today. Used in the majority of US seed rounds.

Eighty‑five percent of the raise goes to growth and product. The remaining fifteen percent covers a fixed cost base of $25,000 per month for the full Vietnam team, infrastructure, and tooling. AI compute is funded by NVIDIA.

Targeted close: 60 to 90 days from first investor conversation.

The MVP is live. The revenue is not yet engineered. Seed capital is the bridge between proof‑of‑product and proof‑of‑revenue.

Chapter Two · Why Now

A Letter from the Chairman of the Board

A note from the Hon. David Wells on the institutional context for this round, the team executing against it, and what the Board is asking of investors who join.
To prospective investors ·

It is unusual for the Chairman of a seed‑stage company to write the opening letter. I am writing this one because the case for ADAMftd does not rest on a single individual or a single product moment. It rests on a category that is being defined right now, in front of us, with material consequences for global trade · and on a team that has built the right thing, at the right time, at remarkable capital efficiency.

I have spent my career adjacent to international trade policy, trade finance, and the institutions that govern cross‑border commerce. The structural problem the company addresses is genuine: a twenty‑five trillion dollar market is being run on tooling designed for compliance teams at money‑centre banks, while the other ninety‑five percent of participants · exporters, importers, customs brokers, mid‑market traders, even export credit agencies serving SMEs · have nothing comparable available at a price they can afford.

What the team has built is, in plain terms, the first credible answer to that problem. The Minimum Viable Product went live at the end of March 2026. Thirteen integrated tools, eighty‑seven capabilities, seven billion verified records, a grounded AI architecture that cites its sources and refuses to invent. In the two months since the MVP launched, nine hundred and sixty‑seven users have signed up from thirty‑plus countries with no marketing spend. Sixty‑four of them consumed every starter credit available to them. That is not a vanity metric. It is a queue.

The reason the Board is bringing this round to market now, rather than after revenue, is straightforward governance. Three things are simultaneously true: the geopolitical moment · Hormuz, Suez, sanctions cascades, tariff escalation · is making trade intelligence existential rather than discretionary; the market expects AI as the native interface to complex data; and NVIDIA has awarded the company one million dollars of compute credits, funding twelve months of infrastructure at zero cash cost. The window to define this category is open. It will not stay open.

The capital being raised · two and a half to four million dollars on a SAFE at a twenty million dollar cap · funds one thing: the deliberate construction of a commercial engine on top of a validated product. Eighty‑five percent goes to growth and product. The operating base is sized lean by design and audited at twenty‑five thousand dollars per month, all‑in. The Board is satisfied that this is the lowest valuation point at which this company will be available to investors.

I am asking you to read the rest of this book with the question a Board director would ask: is the risk that remains worth the entry price. I believe it is. The product risk has been retired. The institutional access is in place. What remains is execution on commercialisation · the risk seed capital is designed to retire · and a clear path to a Series A at three to five times this valuation.

I would welcome the conversation that follows.

David Wells

08.1Why credits, not seats

Seat‑based pricing punishes the customers we want most: SME teams that consume intelligence in bursts, not in eight‑hour shifts on an enterprise data terminal. A trade analyst at a customs broker may run forty queries in a morning and none for three days. A compliance officer at a mid‑market exporter checks counterparties before each shipment. Both are heavy users by volume. Neither would buy a seat.

Credits map cleanly to that reality. They lower friction at the entry point (twenty‑five free credits, no card), and they scale with the value delivered. They also produce the metric Series A investors want: usage that grows independently of headcount, with margin that improves as the engine scales.

What Can Go Wrong

Honest disclosure of the risks an investor should price at this stage, and what the company is doing to mitigate each. This section is not exhaustive; recipients should conduct independent diligence.
Risk 01 · Commercialisation execution
Funded GTM may not convert organic pull into paid revenue at the assumed rate.
Pre‑revenue companies routinely under‑convert. Even with 967 organic users, conversion to paid is a fresh muscle the team has not exercised at scale.
Mitigation Identified power‑user cohort (64 credit‑exhausted users) provides a known first call list. Affiliate programme already operating in 11 countries. SACE pipeline gives an anchor institutional contract independent of SaaS conversion.
Risk 02 · Competitive response
An incumbent data major or a well‑capitalised entrant launches a mid‑market trade product.
Incumbents have data, distribution, and capital. A serious response would compress pricing power and elongate the path to category leadership.
Mitigation Two‑year head start on the integrated five‑layer workspace; ICTTM institutional access; AI architecture built grounded from day one (incumbents must retrofit). The category is defined by speed of land‑grab, which seed capital funds.
Risk 03 · Key‑person dependency
The Chief Executive is the single point of execution across sales, marketing, and product direction.
Giuseppe Funaro, CEO, is 100% involved in every aspect of the business — particularly sales, marketing, product direction, and development. That breadth is an asset today but concentrates execution risk. (The International Trade Council shareholder relationship, by contrast, is held by Tristan Evans, CTO — that institutional access does not sit with the CEO.)
Mitigation Post‑seed plan includes a VP Sales hire and a structured handover of the commercial motion to a dedicated revenue team. The International Trade Council shareholder relationship is corporate, not personal, and sits with the CTO.
Risk 04 · Data quality & trust
A grounded AI product fails if the underlying data is wrong.
Trade data is messy, multi‑source, and conflicting. A single high‑profile error in sanctions or tariff output erodes trust faster than feature debt.
Mitigation Monte Carlo conflict‑resolution pipeline; every answer cites source; ongoing data curation is a named line in the use‑of‑funds. Continuous improvement is funded into the operating plan, not deferred.
Risk 05 · Geopolitical timing
A sharp reversal in trade tensions could compress urgency.
Some of the buying urgency rests on current geopolitical conditions (Hormuz, Suez, tariff escalations). A material de‑escalation would slow institutional buying cycles.
Mitigation The underlying need · integrated multi‑layer trade intelligence at mid‑market pricing · exists with or without acute disruption. Sanctions screening and tariff complexity are structural, not cyclical.
Risk 06 · Capital runway
A delay between seed and Series A creates a financing gap.
If the 12‑to‑18 month Series A target slips, the company will need a bridge.
Mitigation $25k/month operating base is among the lowest in comparable AI infrastructure companies. NVIDIA compute is funded for twelve months. The runway extension levers (pricing, headcount) are well understood and deliverable inside thirty days.

15 · FAQ for Investors
Chapter Fifteen · FAQ

Questions We Receive Most Often

Short, direct answers to the questions that surface repeatedly in investor conversations. The longer answers live in the relevant chapters above.
What is a SAFE, in plain English?
Simple Agreement for Future Equity · the standard Y Combinator seed instrument. You wire capital now; it converts to equity automatically at the next priced round (Series A), at the lower of the valuation cap ($20M post‑money) or the Series A price. No interest, no maturity date, no debt. It avoids the cost and complexity of negotiating share terms at seed and is used in the majority of US seed rounds.
Why pre‑revenue, when you have 967 users in 2 months?
The MVP only went live at the end of March 2026 · two months ago. We deliberately did not turn on the payment funnel during the initial validation window. Charging the first 967 users would have generated noise revenue and depleted goodwill in a community we want to retain. The conversion machinery exists; it has not been switched on.
Why is the operating cost so low?
The full engineering and data team is in Ho Chi Minh City · senior, retained, and delivering at roughly one‑fifth of US benchmark cost. AI infrastructure is funded by the NVIDIA $1M credit award. The $25k/month figure is real and audited.
What if an incumbent data major launches a mid‑market product?
The economics of the large institutional suppliers depend on enterprise seat pricing at $25k+ per year. Down‑market launches would cannibalise their core revenue, so they are structurally disincentivised. Even if one moved, the five‑layer integration plus mid‑market price plus grounded‑native AI architecture is a 24‑month head start.
What is the path to Series A?
Three milestones inside 12 to 18 months: 50+ paying customers with measurable CAC/LTV, the first white‑label portal live, and an anchor ECA (SACE or equivalent) contracted. At that profile, Series A pricing of $60M to $100M is defensible.
Will you take a board seat?
Not required at seed. An observer seat is available on request. Governance is intentionally lean at this stage. Formal board structure will be put in place at Series A.
What does the lead investor get?
A $500k to $1M lead gets pro‑rata rights through Series A, monthly investor letters, direct access to the CEO for strategic introductions, and the option of an observer seat. Lead is the most efficient seat in this round.
Why now, and not after revenue?
Seed is the lowest‑price entry in this company's fundraising journey. Revenue proof will lift Series A pricing to $60M+ · by definition, post‑revenue capital is 3x to 5x more expensive than seed capital. Investors who arrive now capture the entire step‑up.
